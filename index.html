World-anchored whole-body control on the Unitree G1.

Abstract

BeJEPA learns a goal-conditioned behavior world model for humanoid control through unsupervised reinforcement learning from human motion priors and robot interaction. It represents whole-body posture, world position, and heading in a shared latent space. A Dynamic Predictor models immediate action consequences, while a Successor Predictor summarizes expected long-term behavior. Their composition guides policy learning without long imagined rollouts, and Dynamic further refines actions at inference time. BeJEPA improves world-coordinate motion tracking and enables a physical Unitree G1 to reach joint posture and world-pose goals, stand up from the ground, and return after disturbances and goal changes.

Method

A goal in the shared latent space

The shared encoder maps the current state and target to embeddings ztz_tzt​ and zgz_gzg​, capturing posture, world position, and heading. Normalizing them to radius d\sqrt dd​ makes similarity reward equivalent to proximity to the goal:

One physical step, one long-term summary

Dynamic predicts an action's next latent state, independently of the goal. Successor summarizes future embeddings under the goal-conditioned policy, with discount factor γ\gammaγ:

z^t+1=Dν(zt,at)≈Eϕ(st+1)\widehat z_{t+1}=D_\nu(z_t,a_t)\approx E_\phi(s_{t+1})zt+1​=Dν​(zt​,at​)≈Eϕ​(st+1​)
Sξ(zt,zg)≈(1−γ) Eπg ⁣[∑k=0∞γkzt+k | st]S_\xi(z_t,z_g)\approx(1-\gamma)\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_g}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^k z_{t+k}\,\middle|\,s_t\right]Sξ​(zt​,zg​)≈(1−γ)Eπg​​[k=0∑∞​γkzt+k​​st​]

Both train the shared encoder from robot transitions: Dynamic uses observed next states, while Successor uses a vector Bellman target to learn long-term continuation without long imagined rollouts.

Compose the predictions to choose actions

Combining Dynamic's predicted next state with Successor yields a goal-aligned action value. Gradients through both predictors guide the actor toward useful long-term behavior:

Q^g(zt,at)=⟨(1−γ)zt+γSξ(z^t+1,zg), zg⟩\widehat Q_g(z_t,a_t)=\big\langle(1-\gamma)z_t+\gamma S_\xi(\widehat z_{t+1},z_g),\,z_g\big\rangleQ​g​(zt​,at​)=⟨(1−γ)zt​+γSξ​(zt+1​,zg​),zg​⟩

A fixed goal enables reaching; successive goals enable tracking. At deployment, an observation-history encoder supplies the state representation, and Dynamic supports short-horizon MPC refinement with frozen network weights.

02

More precise execution, the same pretrained model

Dynamic-based MPC refines actions at inference time without retraining. The Balanced setting reduces all six tracking errors, with a mean relative reduction of 31.2% on AMASS and 13.4% on LAFAN over BeJEPA. Changing the MPC cost shifts priority toward root translation or wrist orientation.

Closer to the reference clock. Balanced MPC reduces mean signed lag from 94.8 to 21.2 ms on AMASS, and from 97.2 to 51.0 ms on LAFAN, using the same pretrained weights.
03

World-anchored control on the physical G1

On the physical robot, BeJEPA coordinates posture with a persistent world reference: returning after an external kick, standing up from the ground, and switching between goals before returning to the starting location.
